Description

Why do Egyptian figures look so strangely twisted – and why did ancient Egyptian art look almost the same for 3,000 years? Find out with this ancient Egypt reading scavenger hunt! Zoe and Marcus explore an Egyptian art museum with guide Ms. Kaya and their little scarab charm Skara – and your students take the tour with them: from the obelisk to the relief wall, the pharaoh hall, the paint room, the grid wall, the statue hall, and the treasure room with the golden mask of Tutankhamun, all the way to the painted tomb chamber. Students read a short station, find the clue to the next place on a colorful museum map, collect a letter at every correct station, and unscramble the letters to crack the secret word. Dead ends let students check their own work – just like an escape room on paper.

WHAT’S INCLUDED (color + black-and-white, 23 pages each):

  • Reading scavenger hunt in 3 differentiated levels (★ easy, ★★ medium, ★★★ challenging) – same story and map, so you can differentiate in one class
  • Museum map (landscape, full page) with letter badges
  • 3 built-in tasks: quiz, color matching, and a riddle + a math word problem with picture gates
  • Listening comprehension page – students scan the QR code in the PDF to play the recording (MP3) and answer multiple-choice and open questions
  • Puzzle page: word search + short history questions
  • Coloring page
  • Teacher guide (standards, prep, timing, step-by-step, follow-up ideas) and full answer key

HISTORY & ART CONTENT: ancient Egypt as an early civilization – composite view (head in profile, eye and shoulders from the front), hierarchical scale (important people are shown bigger), mineral colors and their meanings, the square grid painters used, frontal stone statues with the left foot forward, gold and lapis lazuli treasures, Tutankhamun’s tomb (discovered in 1922), and tomb paintings made for the afterlife. Connects to world history units on ancient civilizations (C3 Framework D2.His) and to the visual arts standard VA:Cn11 (art in its historical and cultural context). Real works of art are described, not shown.

SKILLS: close reading and key details in informational text (RI.5.1 / RI.6.1), academic vocabulary (RI.5.4 / RI.6.4), following written directions, listening comprehension (SL.5.2 / SL.6.2), addition, subtraction, and multiplication.
