Description

What do a fish, a frog, a turtle, a robin, and a fox have in common – and how are they different? Find out with this vertebrates reading scavenger hunt! Lily and Jamal tour a natural history museum with guide Ms. Spinelli – and your students take the tour with them: from the aquarium to the frog pond, the terrarium, the bird aviary, the mammal hall, the skeleton room, and the egg display, all the way to the discovery table, where they sort animal cards into the five groups. Students read a short station, find the clue to the next place on a colorful museum map, collect a letter at every correct station, and unscramble the letters to crack the secret word. Dead ends let students check their own work – just like an escape room on paper.

WHAT’S INCLUDED (color + black-and-white, 23 pages each):

  • Reading scavenger hunt in 3 differentiated levels (★ easy, ★★ medium, ★★★ challenging) – same story and map, so you can differentiate in one class
  • Museum map (landscape, full page) with letter badges
  • 3 built-in tasks: matching (animal group and trait), quiz, and a riddle + a math word problem with picture gates
  • Listening comprehension page – students scan the QR code in the PDF to play the recording (MP3) and answer multiple-choice and open questions
  • Puzzle page: word search + short science questions
  • Coloring page
  • Teacher guide (standards, prep, timing, step-by-step, follow-up ideas) and full answer key

SCIENCE CONTENT: the five groups of vertebrates and their traits – fish (gills, fins, scales), amphibians (smooth, moist skin, tadpoles, metamorphosis), reptiles (dry, scaly skin, eggs with a shell on land), birds (feathers, wings, beak, hard-shelled eggs), and mammals (fur or hair, milk, most babies born alive – including whales and dolphins); cold-blooded vs. warm-blooded; the spine and inner skeleton all vertebrates share; tricky cases like the penguin and the bat (connects to NGSS 4-LS1-1 and to sorting animals by observable traits).

SKILLS: close reading and key details in informational text (RI.4.1 / RI.5.1), domain-specific vocabulary, following written directions, listening comprehension (SL.4.2 / SL.5.2), addition and multiplication.

PERFECT FOR: animal groups and vertebrate units, life science, museum or zoo field trip prep, science and ELA integration, early finishers, substitute plans, and small groups.

TIME: about 90 minutes (can be split over two lesson
